# Shared contributor and agent contract

## Purpose and authority

Create three independent proposals, then one consistent, repeatable Track C workshop.
Each of Aditya Singh, Dhruv Gupta, and Vinit Shah must be able to deliver the final workshop alone.
Read this file, `docs/workshop-brief.md`, and `templates/workshop-plan.md` before drafting.
These instructions apply to every contributor and agent working in this repository.

The organizer brief defines Track C as **AI for Career & Productivity** for working professionals.
Vinit's planning constraints: phone-only attendees, practical AI familiarity, advance preparation,
and concrete data-sharing and permission risks. The brief's flow is a recommendation; proposed
changes must retain its learning outcomes and explain the tradeoff. Do not drift into Track D's
general no-code building curriculum. Use the three names above; other names in conversational
examples do not add contributors.

## Required outcomes and constraints

Every proposal must enable attendees to:

- Improve a work-related draft by adding context, intent, format, and constraints, then iterating.
- Choose a suitable tool for a task and explain when to verify its answer or avoid using it.
- Map a repetitive task as trigger, AI step, human approval, and destination.
- Recognize unsafe data sharing, excessive permissions, and unreliable or biased output.
- Leave with a practical 30-day plan: three weekly tasks introduced one at a time, a reusable
  prompt, a way to measure improvement, and a way to communicate the result at work.

Design exactly **45 minutes**, including setup, exercises, transitions, and questions, for about
30 people at round tables. One facilitator must be sufficient. Assume iOS and Android phones,
mixed experience, free accounts, and uneven connectivity. Do not require a laptop, terminal,
API key, payment card, paid trial, employer account, or connected work apps for core participation.
Every live activity needs a paper or saved-example fallback. Distinguish attendee requirements
from optional facilitator equipment; do not assume projector or Wi-Fi availability is confirmed.

State a testable workshop hypothesis, measurable exit checks, and what would disconfirm success.
Breadth is secondary to participants completing something useful. Recommend a small required
tool set; place broader exploration in optional follow-up material.

## Tools and preparation

Each proposal must contain a concrete attendee prep checklist and tool matrix: exact product
and official URL, purpose, required/optional/demo-only, iOS/Android/browser access, account and
free-tier limits, setup steps, permissions, data controls, fallback, source, and verification date.
Use official vendor documentation for changing claims. Mark unknowns and distinguish
documentation review from hands-on testing. Do not claim a phone test unless it happened.
Recheck selected tools before the rehearsal and final freeze.

ChatGPT, Claude/Claude Code, Grok/“Grokbot,” and “Muse” are candidate ideas, not approved installs.
Identify the exact product behind ambiguous names; do not invent download links. Distinguish
Claude's chat app from Claude Code. Evaluate phone access and account restrictions before
including coding agents. No OpenAI or other vendor setup is needed merely to write a proposal.
Unverified or unsuitable products cannot become attendee prerequisites.

## Safety must be teachable

Use fictional or synthetic data for every demo. Attendees may describe a real task on paper,
but must sanitize it before entering anything into a tool. Never solicit employer/client records,
personal identifiers, financial records, credentials, unreleased work, or confidential documents.
Employer policy still applies; a paid or enterprise label is not automatic permission to upload.

Include at least two short, reproducible live examples with exact inputs, expected lessons,
debrief questions, and saved fallbacks. Cover data oversharing and excessive permissions;
also teach fact-checking, bias, and human accountability. Use mock permission screens or
synthetic scenarios rather than granting live access to mail, files, contacts, or calendars.
Show what to deny, narrow, or revoke. Do not authorize purchases, sends, public sharing,
account connections, or real automation during a demo. Explain that privacy settings,
retention, training use, and shared-link visibility are separate questions to verify.

main plan 1-3 pages. Link optional supporting assets, but include runnable prompts and essential

private meeting notes, or third-party source dumps. Do not push or publish unless the human
has authorized it. No participant outreach or organizer messages are authorized by this contract.

Consolidation requires an explicit human request. Compare segments using `final/selection.md`,
record selection reasons and source proposals, then compose a coherent `final/plan.md` rather
than concatenating all three. Coordinators jointly confirm the selection; an agent cannot invent
their agreement. Unresolved checks keep the final plan labeled Draft. Each coordinator must
rehearse the same version independently before it becomes Ready.
